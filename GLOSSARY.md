# Software workflow research

The language of the human-driven software development workflows studied in this repository.

## Language

**Human-driven workflow**:
A development process in which a human initiates each stage and owns product decisions and transitions, while AI performs the requested work within that stage.

**Stage**:
A bounded portion of the workflow that the human starts with a defined purpose and scope. A stage may include research, drafting, coding, testing, or review.

**Entry path**:
The starting context a developer brings to the workflow, such as a raw idea, an existing PRD, a feature request, or a routine bug fix.

**Planning route**:
The sequence of planning stages needed to make a particular request ready for implementation, based on its unresolved decisions and their consequences.

**Acceptance evidence**:
Observable results used to determine whether a requested outcome has been achieved.

**Stage authorization**:
The scope, constraints, and permitted actions granted by a human for a particular stage.

**Session handoff**:
A record of a session’s decisions, artifacts, evidence, unfinished work, and next action that lets a later session or collaborator continue reliably.

**Artifact authority**:
The concern for which an accepted artifact is the source of intended truth in the workflow.

**Artifact approval**:
A human’s recorded acceptance of a particular artifact revision and its meaning.

**Stale work**:
Work or approval evidence whose supporting requirements, decisions, or skill contracts have changed and whose continued validity has not yet been established.

**Framing PRD**:
An initial statement of the problem, intended audience, desired outcomes, and boundaries, with evidence, assumptions, and unresolved questions made explicit. Human acceptance establishes direction for further planning.

**Implementation readiness**:
The state in which the specification and acceptance criteria are sufficiently settled and accepted for implementation to begin within the authorized scope.

**Design readiness**:
The state in which the intended user experience is sufficiently settled and accepted for a defined implementation scope.

**Design package**:
The reviewable collection of design evidence describing the intended user experience for a defined scope.
